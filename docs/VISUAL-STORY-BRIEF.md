## Page order (only this)

1. **Problem.** One headline. One sentence. One full-bleed picture of the line (paid Manhattan vs Mott Haven, ~4 miles north). No lede stack. No pathline.
2. **Datasets, with photos.** One plate per file. Photo or diagram is the plate. Name + one line under it. Not a method essay.
3. **What we found.** Four to six huge graphs. One sentence under each. Then two pictures: street kit now; Deegan lid later.

A 12-year-old or limited-English reader should finish without a glossary.

---

## Image-first

- The picture is the sentence. Type only names what to look at.
- One idea per screen. One hero image. Side images only if they are silent (no captions).
- Prefer `story-art/` plates if a sibling wrote them. Else enlarge `paper/figures/fig1-gates.svg` … `fig5-econ.svg`. Photos from `paper/refs/` or `story-art/refs/` (public only).
- Mobile first. Image taller than the type. White / near-white. Tiny ink. One accent (crimson) used rarely.
- Map is a picture or it is gone. Not a dashboard.

## Words

Least words. Grade-6. Subject first. Contractions. No em dash. No "imagine if." No `like` / `hey`. Neighborhood names (Mott Haven), not cold codes.

**Close (two pictures, two lines):**
- Street now: trees, curb, rain gardens. NYC already pays for this kind of job.
- Highway park later: big, slow, $250M–$1B+. Not next year's Parks contract.

Do not sell the lid as the first ask.
