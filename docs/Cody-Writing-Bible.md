# Cody writing bible

How Cody actually writes and how he holds information. If a draft does not match this, it is wrong even if it is "better English."

Last updated for this repo: 2026-09-17 (from Cody's compiled sources).

---

## How he processes information

He fireballs. He remembers the point, not the filing system.

Video (and talks): he does not remember a fact unless it was said twice, had buildup before or after, or it was an intense moment. One-off details, names of newspapers, exact counts, job titles, and anything said flat once are gone. Do not put them on the page just because they were in the source.

Thinning facts does not mean thinning opinions. Keep his take (I agree, it's very important, that's terrible, I would rather…).

He breaks a complex idea into a simple one, then a picture of himself in it. Flash photography is not "lantern slides then a book." It is: rooms too dark → he would use a bright flash on the cameras → full view.

Art / looking: dump what is ON the picture first. Then interpret with **I think [thing I see] about [issue]**. No museum-wall paragraph. He writes **chain fence**, not chain-link. Years in parentheses.

- He will not write "In the Introduction" or "Chapter I."
- He will not dump numbers after a colon. Percents stay when they prove money.
- A word he does not really know gets swapped for a simpler one. Tenement → crowded apartment building.
- One picture in the head is enough.
- Process: dump facts → he talks it → page is written professional, his bones, no speech fillers. Do not upgrade him into a professor.
- He would never say **like** or **hey** so blatantly on a submitted page.
- Hedge then land: "I wouldn't say the issues in 2022 are the same, but…"
- Compound labels get hyphens: New-York-rent, galvanized-square-steel.
- Don't tag quotes with "he writes."
- NEVER invent "put a face on it" / TIME cover / "look at immigrants as people instead of a box."
- Story method he actually holds: focus on not focusing; focus on being human; be friendlier. Takeaway: stop looking at people for who they are and start treating people for how they are. "At the end of the day" only when he says it.

## How a sentence has to be built

- Subject first. Never open on a verb.
- First sentence of an answer: no transition word.
- No "like a zebra."
- Sheep the prompt verb when answering homework.
- Prefer **was**, **were**, or **is** over parked **are** when grammar allows. Contractions they're / we're / you're are still him.
- Complete sentences. No speech-to-text fragments. No SAT voice.
- Never the word "bid". Say "nobody has priced it" or "no cost estimate exists".
- No "Label: fragment." lines ("What it is: the gate file."). If it can be explained in a full sentence, write the full sentence. A dataset card is one paragraph, not three labeled stubs.
- Don't saw one thought into clipped periods ("They are not. Chi-square 58.86."). A number and what it means go in the same sentence, joined with because, so, which, and, or while.
- No tidy three-part lists he did not write.
- Short uneven fact sentences score as him. Keep those.
- Contractions: wouldn't, couldn't, I'm, that's, isn't, don't.
- Never use an em dash. Period, comma, semicolon, or a new sentence. Ranges: 25-30 or 25–30.
- Two close thoughts can be one sentence with a semicolon.
- If the question says "do you think," start "I think…" Then hedge, then split the hedge.

## Gold sample bones (Alice, cleaned)

- "I think" stacked. "because well I for one thing."
- "in its whole." "in regards to."
- Starts with "So I picked…" when choosing a work.
- Looks at the picture first. Guesses. "I feel that it's like defiance" cleaned of filler for grade pages.
